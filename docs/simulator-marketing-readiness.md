# Indoor golf search and SEM readiness

Prepared 2026-10-07 for the national simulator path. Scope is a secondary homepage call to action, `/golf-simulators`, and `/guides/booking-indoor-golf`. This is a content and measurement brief; it does not authorize an ad campaign, spend, a new local page, or a claim that every discovered venue can be monitored.

## Decision and promise

- **Primary landing:** `/golf-simulators` answers rental and indoor-golf searches, explains the one-hour simulator alert, and sends the visitor to `/search?mode=SIMULATOR` to choose a location, date, and time window. Keep the homepage's outdoor golf promise primary.
- **Supporting guide:** `/guides/booking-indoor-golf` explains how to find public rentals, check duration and venue rules, use an alert, and book directly through the official venue. Link both pages to each other and from relevant existing navigation or guide surfaces. These are the only two new content pages in this scope.
- **Truthful boundary:** Tee Time Spot can show likely simulator venues nationwide and save demand before monitoring support exists. A matching email requires verified public rental access, current official source evidence, and a complete supported one-hour session. The golfer follows the official booking page and books direct. A discovered venue, saved alert, or unsupported booking system must never be presented as a confirmed opening or guaranteed monitoring. Simulator rental qualification belongs to `CourseOffering` and its official evidence, independently of outdoor `Course.isPublic` or legacy outdoor hub qualification.

## Search intent and draft ad inventory

These are candidate themes, not observed volume or approved live ads. Start with tight exact/phrase intent and inspect actual terms before expanding. All paid clicks would land on `/golf-simulators`, with its simulator search action; informational searches may also reach the guide organically. Google Ads recommends using the [search terms report to refine negative keywords](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7102466?hl=en).

| Intent group | Example candidate terms | Landing and honest answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public bay rental nearby | `golf simulator rental near me`, `rent golf simulator bay`, `golf simulator hourly rental` | `/golf-simulators`: discover likely public rentals, set a one-hour alert, and book on the official venue site. |
| Indoor golf nearby | `indoor golf near me`, `indoor golf simulator near me`, `indoor golf bay rental` | `/golf-simulators`: clarify that this is venue discovery and availability alerts, not a venue or instant booking engine. |
| Availability help | `golf simulator availability alerts`, `indoor golf booking alerts`, `simulator bay openings` | `/golf-simulators`: explain supported public openings, email notification, and direct official booking. The guide supplies deeper explanation. |

Draft Search ad assets for review (headlines under 30 characters; descriptions under 90): **“Golf Simulator Alerts”** / **“Find Indoor Golf Near You”** / **“You Book Direct”**. Descriptions: **“Find nearby indoor golf. Get opening alerts at supported venues. You book direct.”** and **“Set a one-hour bay alert. Get an official link when a supported opening appears.”** Do not use “book now,” “instant availability,” “all venues monitored,” national coverage guarantees, or a venue name without evidence. Ensure the destination repeats these limits: Google requires [clear, honest ad and destination claims](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955?hl=en).

Initial negative themes to review by match type: simulator **hardware for sale**, launch monitors, projectors, screens, mats, **DIY/home setup**, garage builds, software downloads, repairs, wholesale, franchise and employment. Review lessons, coaching, fitting, and private/member-only queries against the actual offering; they are generally outside public hourly rental intent. Do not broadly exclude `golf`, `indoor`, or `simulator`, and check negatives against valuable long-tail searches. After any approved campaign starts, use real search terms to adjust the list; there are no campaign reports available in this brief.

## Read-only baseline before the pages deploy

Public HTTP checks on 2026-10-07: `/` returned 200 with a public outdoor tee-time title, description, canonical, and `index, follow` robots meta. Its HTML had a Google site-verification meta tag, but that tag alone does **not** establish current Search Console ownership or reporting access. `/robots.txt` returned 200, allowed `/`, disallowed `/api/`, and named `/sitemap.xml`. The sitemap returned 200 with 174 `<loc>` entries and no planned simulator-page URL. Both `/golf-simulators` and `/guides/booking-indoor-golf` returned 404; the homepage HTML had no links to either. This is the **prechange production baseline**, not a judgment of the new branch or post-deployment indexability.

After deployment, fetch both URLs and verify 200, distinct descriptive title/description/H1, self-canonical, index/follow, readable server HTML, links from the homepage and guide index as appropriate, links between the two pages, and both canonical URLs in the live sitemap. Recheck `robots.txt` and use Search Console URL Inspection and the Sitemaps/Performance reports **if property access is provided**. A 200, sitemap entry, or verification meta tag is not proof of Google indexing or ranking. Google advises [descriptive page metadata and crawlable content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/get-started-developers) and says [sitemap submission is a hint, not an indexing guarantee](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap).

This worktree and the available tools do not provide authenticated Search Console property reports, Google Ads account reports, or Keyword Planner outputs. Their ownership, account state, existing campaigns, impressions, clicks, query rankings, volume, CPC, and conversions are **unverified**, not assumed absent or zero. Ask an account owner for read-only exports or access before setting numeric targets or a budget. Google Search Console's [Performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en) is the source for page/query clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position; Keyword Planner's [location-dependent historical metrics and forecasts](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en) are inputs for future geo and budget decisions, not observed Tee Time Spot demand.

## Measurement without invented attribution

Current `WebsiteEvent` tracking can aggregate page views and funnel events by path, `PUBLIC` traffic class, optional `OUTDOOR`/`SIMULATOR` metadata, and a coarse discovery-source label such as `SEARCH_GOOGLE`. Client events use beacon or best-effort fetch and may be missed. The label comes from referrer/session storage; it does **not** separate paid from organic Google traffic and is not an ad conversion signal. Do not infer unique people, paid return, or bookings from it. Durable `TeeSearch.mode=SIMULATOR` with `trafficClass=PUBLIC` can corroborate saved simulator alert totals, including status, while still not proving ad attribution, monitored coverage, or completed venue bookings. Report page views, simulator search starts/submissions, saved public simulator alerts, current support/notification outcomes, and Search Console metrics as separate measures; avoid a fabricated conversion rate across incompatible sources.

Before any spend decision, request Keyword Planner estimates for the intent groups at **US national and region-level geographies** (same language/network/date window) and read-only Search Console page/query baseline. Compare regional estimates with actual offering evidence and monitoring support; choose geography only after that review. Do not invent search volume, CPC, ranking, or expected return. No campaign launch or budget is proposed here.
